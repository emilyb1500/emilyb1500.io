Cat Behavior Research

Insightful studies from SCAA and trusted partners

Feed Response Study (2000)

Coming from our own personal research at SCAA, we observed 100 cats over a 5-month period in their homes and studied their response times when it came to feeding time.

We discovered that 100% of cats arrived at their food bowls within 5 minutes of feeding time. However, when cats were called outside of their main feeding time, only 10% responded to our calls.

This not only suggests that cats can understand time-related routines, but that they selectively choose to acknowledge us based on how they feel.

The Name Game (2023)

Research from the Feline Institute provided information about how cats understand their names. They can hear us when we call them from all the way across the house, but still choose not to acknowledge us.

In 100% of the cases, cats responded to hearing their owners call them by signaling through their body language—ear movements, tail flicks, and head tilts. However, only about 20% of cats chose to move toward their owners.

This research supports the idea that cats understand their names—they’re just simply choosing to ignore us.

Selective Attention Experiment (2022)

This study set out to prove that cats can hear our commands, understand them, but still choose not to respond simply because they didn’t feel like getting up.

Owners were asked to call their cats under different conditions:

  • Food in hand: 90% responded
  • Empty hand: 10% responded
  • Stranger calling: 2% responded
  • Vet in the room: 0% responded

Video analysis showed cats physically reacting (ear twitching, tail flicking) but still refusing to acknowledge the requests. The pretend ignorance played a huge role in our understanding of human-feline relationships—further supporting the intelligence of our cats.
